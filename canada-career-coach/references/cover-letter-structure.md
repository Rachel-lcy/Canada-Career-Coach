# Cover Letter Structure

## Gate: ask first

Do not draft until the user has answered:

1. Organization and role title.
2. What specifically interests them about this organization or role.
3. Which experience or angle they want to lead with.

## Five-paragraph structure (about 300–450 words, one letter-size page)

1. **Opening with a point of view.** Not "I am applying for…". Name the role, then one sentence on why this work matters to you. Prompt: _What do you believe about this kind of work that a hiring manager might not expect?_
2. **How you work.** The principle behind how you work. Prompt: _Where has that principle changed an outcome?_
3. **Evidence.** Two or three concrete examples with scope and a verifiable result. Prompt: _Which achievements from your profile map most directly to the posting's top three requirements?_
4. **Why this organization and role.** Specific, researched, not interchangeable with any other employer. Prompt: _What did you learn about them that is not on the first page of their website (their community work, a recent project, their values statement)?_
5. **Short, confident close.** Thank the reader and invite a conversation, without grovelling. Prompt: _What do you want to be doing in this role in a year?_

## Format rules

- Address a named person when the posting or a quick search gives one; otherwise "Dear Hiring Team" is fine in Canada.
- No generic openers and no restating the resume line by line.
- Mirror the posting's vocabulary only where the user has the experience.
- Warm but professional; active voice; Canadian spelling.
- Default coach output is an outline plus prompts. Provide full prose only if asked, and mark it as a draft for the user to rewrite.
